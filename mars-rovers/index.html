The Mars Exploration Program

Welcome to the Red Planet

For decades, NASA has sent robotic explorers across Mars to study its rocks, climate, and history. Each rover helps scientists understand whether the planet once supported conditions suitable for life.

From the early Spirit and Opportunity missions to Curiosity and Perseverance, every new rover builds on discoveries made by the last. Explore the missions below and learn more about their history.

Meet the Rovers

Curiosity

Launched November 26, 2011, and landed August 6, 2012. Curiosity studies the geology and past habitability of Gale Crater. Its onboard laboratory has identified organic molecules in Martian rocks.

Learn about Curiosity

Perseverance

Launched July 30, 2020, and landed February 18, 2021. Perseverance explores Jezero Crater, studying ancient environments and collecting rock samples for possible future return to Earth.

Learn about Perseverance

Why Explore Mars?

Mars preserves clues about water, changing climates, and the history of our solar system. Rovers allow scientists to investigate the surface in ways orbiters cannot.

Read common questions

How Exploration Has Changed

The original Mars rovers were small, solar-powered explorers. Later rovers gained advanced cameras, drills, and scientific instruments. Curiosity and Perseverance use radioisotope power systems to continue working through cold Martian seasons.
