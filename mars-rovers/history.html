A History of Exploring Mars

Every rover has made the next mission possible, from finding evidence of past water to testing new tools for planetary exploration.

Spirit

Launched June 10, 2003 | Landed January 4, 2004

Spirit was one of two Mars Exploration Rovers sent to search for evidence that water once shaped Mars. It explored Gusev Crater, finding evidence that some rocks had been altered by water.

Design: Spirit used solar panels, six wheels, panoramic cameras, a rock abrasion tool, and instruments for examining minerals. Its mission lasted far longer than the planned 90 Martian days.

Opportunity

Launched July 7, 2003 | Landed January 25, 2004

Opportunity investigated Meridiani Planum and found strong evidence for ancient water, including mineral deposits and sedimentary rock features. It traveled for nearly 15 years before its mission ended in 2018.

Design: Like Spirit, Opportunity was a solar-powered six-wheeled rover equipped with cameras and tools for studying the composition and texture of rocks.

Curiosity

Launched November 26, 2011 | Landed August 6, 2012

Curiosity's goal is to study whether Gale Crater could once have supported microbial life. The rover found evidence of ancient lakes, organic molecules, and changing environmental conditions.

Design: This car-sized, six-wheeled rover carries a drill, cameras, a laser spectrometer, and a laboratory that analyzes samples. It is powered by a radioisotope generator rather than solar panels.

Perseverance

Launched July 30, 2020 | Landed February 18, 2021

Perseverance explores Jezero Crater, the location of an ancient lake and river delta. Its mission is to investigate signs of ancient microbial life and collect rock and soil samples that could be brought to Earth on a future mission.

Design: Its instruments include cameras, a rock-coring drill, advanced analytical tools, and a system for storing sample tubes. It also carried the Ingenuity helicopter, which completed the first powered flights on another planet.
