Questions and answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Here are some common questions about the rovers and their missions on Mars.

Why does NASA send rovers to Mars?

Rovers allow scientists to take close-up pictures, examine rocks, and test soil directly on the surface. These observations help researchers understand Mars' history and search for evidence of environments that may once have supported life.

How do rovers get to the surface?

They travel through space aboard spacecraft. At Mars, a combination of atmospheric entry, parachutes, and landing systems slows them down. Curiosity and Perseverance used a powered descent stage to lower the rover on cables.

What powers a Mars rover?

Spirit and Opportunity used solar panels. Curiosity and Perseverance use a radioisotope power system that converts heat from radioactive decay into electricity.

Have the rovers found life?

No Mars rover has confirmed the discovery of life. Rovers have found evidence of ancient water and organic compounds, which help scientists assess whether Mars once had habitable environments.

Can someone drive a rover like a remote-control car?

Not in real time. The distance between Earth and Mars creates a delay in radio communications. Mission teams plan and send commands, and the rovers can also make some navigation decisions on their own.
